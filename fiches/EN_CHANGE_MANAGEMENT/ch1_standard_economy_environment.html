Change Management for Sustainability

Chapter 1 — The Standard Economy and the Environment

Introduction: The Need for Transition
  • Global GDP: $8T (1944) → $60T (2000) → $111T (2024) — exponential growth
  • Flip side: 3/4 of land and 2/3 of oceans degraded
  • Richest 10% responsible for 2/3 of warming since 1990 (Nature Climate Change, 2025)
  • We are in an era of "bad-growth": presentism, excess, precarity
  • Only 57 out of 77,000 top journal articles addressed climate change (<0.1%)
  • Stern & Oswald (2019): "Economists are letting the world down"
Definition
Transition (Latin transitio): A passage that is both gradual and profound — not an adjustment nor a revolution, but a shift from one equilibrium to another requiring political and social innovation.
I. Economics as the Science of Moderation
Definition
Oikonomia = oikos (home) + nomos (law). Aristotle: economics = discipline of moderation for satisfying basic human needs. Goal: social optimum.
  • Ancient agreement: economy must be based on moral values (fair prices, condemnation of greed)
  • Over centuries, rise of individualism replaced social optimum with individual optimum
Physiocrats (c. 1750)
  • Physiocracy = physis (nature) + kratos (power) = "government by nature"
  • Only agriculture creates net wealth; industry/commerce are "sterile"
  • Quesnay (1758): first economic table (precursor to national accounting)
  • Precursors of liberalism: "Let men act and let goods pass"
  • Still relevant: competition over water, fossil fuels, rare earths

Warning
Classical blind spot: nature acquires value only through human labour. Earth's resources considered unlimited.
B. Neoclassical School
  • Broke with philosophy, ethics, justice → became mathematical science
  • Marginal utility: value = utility of last unit consumed (decreasing)
  • Homo economicus: selfish, rational, maximizer
  • Preferences: rational, complete, transitive, non-satiated
  • Cost-benefit analysis: every decision = costs vs. benefits
  • Budget constraints ignore ecological factors (pollution, habitat loss)
Q: Does the Dictator Game support the homo economicus model? No. Most people give money to strangers, showing altruism exists beyond self-interest. The Global Preferences Survey confirms cultural variation in economic preferences across 70 countries.
Pure and Perfect Competition (PPC)
  • 5 conditions: atomicity, free entry/exit, homogeneity, transparency, factor mobility
  • General equilibrium: S = D in all markets simultaneously
  • Pareto optimum: cannot improve one without worsening another (not necessarily fair)
  • In reality: PPC rarely holds → market failures are omnipresent
C. Market Failures
Definition
Externality: effect (+ or -) on third parties not reflected in market price. Negative: pollution, depletion. Positive: innovation, vaccination. Government corrects via taxes, subsidies, regulation (Pigou, 1920).
Definition
Public good: non-rival + non-excludable. Free rider problem: people consume without paying. Solutions: taxation, PPPs (REDD+), collective management (Ostrom, 1990).

Critical Point
Heterodox view: externalities are not just technical problems but symptoms of deeper systemic issues. Economy is embedded in social and ecological systems.
D. Keynesian School
  • Keynes observed 1930s crisis: markets alone do not reach optimum
  • Short-term bias: "In the long run, we are all dead" → prosperity vs. posterity
  • Effective demand determines production, investment, employment
  • GDP growth became top priority → more consumption = more strain on planet
Q: What is the fundamental problem with the mainstream economic approach to the environment? Nature is treated as having no intrinsic value. Classical economists see resources as "free" and unlimited; neoclassical models ignore ecological constraints; Keynesian focus on short-term growth drives overconsumption.
Q: What solution did Elinor Ostrom (1990) propose for managing common goods? Collective management by local communities with clear rules, enforcement mechanisms, and trust in self-governance structures — an alternative to both privatization and state control.
