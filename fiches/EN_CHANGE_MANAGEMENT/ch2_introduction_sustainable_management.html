The Decoupling Challenge (Kaya Identity)
Formula
CO2 = Pop × (GDP/Pop) × (Energy/GDP) × (CO2/Energy)
Managers control only: energy intensity and carbon intensity.
Goal: absolute decoupling — reduce total emissions while growing economically.
What Drives Real Improvement
  • Cleaner Production: Prevent pollution at source, not treat afterward
  • Learning Curves: Costs drop ~20% every time cumulative output doubles
  • Governance & Incentives: Link pay to auditable environmental metrics
Case: Orsted's Transformation
  • 2006: 85% fossil-dependent (DONG Energy); ~1/3 of Denmark's CO2
  • 2014: oil price collapse exposed fossil assets as risky
  • Strategic shift: sold fossil plants, invested in offshore wind
  • 2019: 86% renewables; profits UP; #1 Global 100 Sustainability Index (2020)
Definition
Stranded asset: investment that loses value earlier than expected due to environmental, market, or policy change. NPV collapses when cash flows fall or risk rises.

Sustainability: Definition & Dimensions
Definition
Brundtland (1987): "Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet their own needs."
Triple Bottom Line: People + Planet + Profit — interdependent, not separate.
Diagnosing Unsustainability
  • Fossil fuel use: 8x since 1950; global energy still ~80% fossil
  • CO2 emissions: 37 billion tonnes in 2023 (record high); >420 ppm
  • ~50% of habitable land converted for agriculture
  • Planetary boundaries (Rockstrom, 2009): several already transgressed (climate, biodiversity, nutrient cycles)
  • Strong sustainability: natural capital is non-substitutable
Warning
Greenwashing: Many firms use sustainability branding without changing core operations. Credibility crisis = abundant discourse, little decarbonization.

Circular Economy
  • Linear: Take → Make → Use → Dispose (assumes infinite resources)
  • Circular: Reuse, Remanufacture, Recycle, Regenerate
  • Central ratio: Resource Productivity = Value Added / Material Input
  • Cradle-to-Cradle: waste = food; Ellen MacArthur: biological vs. technical loops
Rethinking Growth
  • Tim Jackson: well-being = social trust + meaning, not consumption volume
  • Kate Raworth: "Doughnut" = safe space between social foundation & ecological ceiling
  • Herman Daly: steady-state economy — quantitative limits, qualitative improvement

Q: What differentiates peripheral from strategic sustainability? Peripheral = isolated CSR, donations, symbolic (reputation management). Strategic = embedded in core operations, product design, procurement (value creation under ecological constraints).
Q: What is absolute decoupling? Reducing total emissions in absolute terms while the economy continues to grow — not just improving the ratio of emissions per unit of GDP.
Q: What key lesson does the Orsted case teach? Sustainability is smart strategy when markets, technology, and regulation align toward low-carbon systems. Leadership commitment + strategic reallocation + governance & measurement = successful transformation.
