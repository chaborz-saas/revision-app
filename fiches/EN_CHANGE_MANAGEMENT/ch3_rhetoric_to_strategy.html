Key Principle
Strategic sustainability lies where business advantage and societal progress converge.
Materiality Analysis: Three Filters
  • Influence: Where can the firm shape outcomes?
  • Risk Exposure: Where are the biggest threats?
  • Impact Potential: Which actions yield both societal and business value?
Definition
Double Materiality = Financial materiality (sustainability → firm finances) + Environmental materiality (firm → environment). Tools: GRI, SASB materiality mapping. Resources allocated where leverage and impact overlap.

Common Flaw
These answer why to act, not how to create value. Result: fragmented CSR with weak authority.
From CSR → ESG → CSM
  • CSR: Voluntary and narrative
  • ESG: Investor compliance and reporting
  • CSM: Data-driven strategic management (measure → manage → improve)
Three Cumulative Stages
  • 1. Moral awareness → recognize imperatives
  • 2. Measurement → quantify with indicators
  • 3. Managerial integration → embed in budgets, KPIs, decisions

Virtuous Circle
Solving societal problems → innovation → lower costs → reputation & license to operate. Constraint = design brief for advantage.

Inside-Out vs. Outside-In
  • Inside-Out: Reduce harms from operations (waste, emissions, labour)
  • Outside-In: Address external conditions affecting competitiveness (infrastructure, health, water)
  • Strategic integration: which issues are most material? How does solving them enhance performance?
Value-Chain Analytics Tools
  • LCA: Lifecycle assessment — quantify impacts across full lifecycle
  • Supply-chain mapping: Identify high-risk inputs, routes, geographies
  • Scope 3 accounting: Upstream + downstream emissions
  • EU CSRD: mandatory disclosure — early movers shape standards
Case Studies
Case: HUL Project Shakti
Trained 120,000+ rural women in India as micro-entrepreneurs. Social: income, empowerment. Strategic: rural distribution reach. Inclusion = supply-chain innovation.
Case: Facebook & Cambridge Analytica
Data misuse, weak governance of privacy & misinformation. Cost: regulatory backlash, trust loss. Sustainability includes digital responsibility.
Q: What is double materiality? It combines financial materiality (how sustainability issues affect the firm's finances) with environmental materiality (how the firm's operations affect the environment). Both directions matter.
Q: How does climate change affect Best Buy, Delta, and ExxonMobil differently? Best Buy: generic issue (store efficiency). Delta: value-chain issue (fleet, fuels). ExxonMobil: competitive-context issue (core business model at risk). Strategy must be firm-specific.
Q: What are the three waves of corporate sustainability? 1) Profit maximization → 2) CSR risk control → 3) Shared value creation. The third wave treats constraints as design briefs for competitive advantage.
Q: Name the four key takeaways of this chapter. 1. Materiality is contextual. 2. Measurement enables focus. 3. Early action creates advantage. 4. Externalities = markets (problems become innovations).
