Change Management for Sustainability

Chapter 4 — Organizational and Institutional Change

Three Barriers to Sustainability
BarrierRoot CauseManagerial Response
Market failuresUnpriced externalities, delayed payback, distorted ROIInternal carbon pricing, shadow costs, composite indices
Behavioral biasesBounded rationality, status-quo bias, risk aversion, framingReframe as experiment; reward learning
Organizational inertiaSilos, quarterly incentives, weak sustainability authorityIntegrate KPIs into core processes & budgeting
Key Concept
Capability-intention gap: Firms want to be sustainable but lack structural capacity. Desire without organizational design = no results.
Definition
Organizational myopia: Short-term focus driven by behavioral biases, causing missed innovation windows. Remedy: reframe sustainability as experimentation and reward learning, not only immediate ROI.
Rana Plaza (2013) — Systemic Failure
  • 1,100+ garment workers killed in Bangladesh building collapse
  • Causes: unsafe buildings, weak regulation, retailer cost-cutting
  • Outsourced risks return as reputation loss & supply disruption
  • Lesson: Voluntary CSR ≠ protection; need regulation + auditing + collective action
The "Defect" Concept (Lean/Six Sigma → Sustainability)
  • Defect = any deviation causing waste or harm (CO2, injury, toxic release)
  • Improvement half-life: time to halve a defect → measures learning rate
  • Each defect = learning opportunity → cut waste, build capability, innovate
Capability Traps (Peter Senge)
PathDescriptionOutcome
B4 — Working harderShort-term output up, capacity downBurnout, depletion
B5 — Working smarterInvest in training, process redesignSustained performance
Escape Route
Build a learning organization. Invest in process improvement even when results are delayed. Sustainability = continuous reinvestment, not a one-off project.
System Archetypes (Meadows & Senge)
LoopEffectExample
Reinforcing (R)Positive feedback: success breeds successInvestment → innovation → profit → reinvestment
Balancing (B)Negative feedback: resists changeFear, budget cuts, resistance, fatigue
Critical Point
Change fails when balancing loops dominate. Task: amplify reinforcing loops, weaken drag forces.
Leadership Intensity = Key Predictor
  • Sector and size do NOT predict sustainability success
  • Leadership commitment: CEO/Board visibility and accountability
  • Cross-functional integration: Sustainability in procurement, R&D, HR, finance
  • Measurement systems: Quantified ESG KPIs linked to incentives
Strategic Transformation Model
  • Progression: Efficiency → Resilience → Regeneration
  • Compete through collaboration and system design, not only price
  • Reward foresight, not short-termism
Three Managerial Imperatives
  • 1. Identify leverage points (small change → big system effect)
  • 2. Align incentives with long-term value
  • 3. Design adaptive systems that learn
Formula
In the 21st century, strategy = sustainability. Sustainability is a management system, not a movement.

Q: What is the "capability-intention gap"? The disconnect between a firm's desire to be sustainable and its structural capacity to execute. Caused by siloed departments, short-term incentives, and weak sustainability authority.
Q: What is the difference between working harder (B4) and working smarter (B5)? B4 (harder): increases short-term output but depletes long-term capacity (burnout). B5 (smarter): invests in training and process redesign for sustained performance. Most firms are trapped in B4.
Q: What lesson does Rana Plaza teach about supply chain management? Outsourced risks return as reputation loss and supply disruption. Voluntary CSR is insufficient; systemic change requires institutional complementarity (regulation, auditing, collective action).
Q: What three factors predict sustainability maturity? 1. Leadership commitment (CEO/Board). 2. Cross-functional integration (not siloed). 3. Measurement systems (quantified KPIs linked to incentives). Sector/size are NOT predictors.
