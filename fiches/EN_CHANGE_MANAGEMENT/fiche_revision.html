📚 Key Notions to Master for the Exam

Based on the Phoenix Organic mock exam · 4 questions, choose 2 · 10–12 lines max
Expected structure: Theory + author/year → Evidence from Phoenix case → Critical reflection (tension/limit). Avoid pure definitions. For 9–10: structured argument + critical nuances.
🔵 Leadership & Change Management
Transformational Leadership — 4I
Burns (1978) · Bass (1985)
A leader who transforms followers using 4 levers: Idealised Influence (charisma, role model) · Inspirational Motivation (compelling vision) · Intellectual Stimulation (challenge, creativity) · Individualised Consideration (personalised attention).
→ Q1: Morrison = 4I. Refusing plastic = Idealised Influence. Natural Step = Intellectual Stimulation. Staff trips = Individualised Consideration.
Kotter — 8-Step Change Model
Kotter (1996)
Linear process: Urgency → Coalition → Vision → Communicate → Empower → Quick wins → Consolidate → Anchor.
→ Q1: Phoenix embodies steps 1–3 from founding. Key contrast: Kotter = crisis-triggered change; Phoenix = proactive/identity-driven from day one.
Lewin's Force Field Analysis
Lewin (1951)
3 phases: Unfreeze → Change → Refreeze. Driving forces vs restraining forces.
→ Q2: Phoenix unfreezes the café channel but the mainstream stays "frozen" (Coca-Cola, supermarkets). Refreezing is unresolved — critical point to mention.
🟣 Institutional & Strategic Theories
Institutional Isomorphism
DiMaggio & Powell (1983)
3 mechanisms pushing organisations to converge: Coercive (legal/client pressure) · Mimetic (copying leaders) · Normative (professional norms).
→ Q2: Supermarkets = coercive. Coca-Cola entering organic = mimetic. "Organic ghettos" = normative. Phoenix = institutional entrepreneur bypassing these pressures.
VRIO
Barney (1991)
A resource creates sustained advantage if: Valuable · Rare · Inimitable · Organized.
→ Q1 & Q4: Phoenix's identity is VRIO (inimitable — embedded in founders' history). Paradox in Q3: if Phoenix spreads its practices → they lose rarity.
Carroll's CSR Pyramid
Carroll (1991)
4 levels: Economic → Legal → Ethical → Philanthropic. Most firms start with economics.
→ Phoenix inverts the logic: ethical/philanthropic first, constraining the economic. Legal ≠ Ethical = greenwashing alert (Q4).
Stakeholder Theory
Freeman (1984)
The firm has obligations to all stakeholders (not just shareholders): employees, suppliers, customers, communities.
→ Q3: Long-term supplier/café relationships = stakeholder strategy → multiplier effect on the supply side.
Paradox Theory
Smith & Lewis (2011)
Organisations face paradoxical tensions. The "both/and" response (embracing both simultaneously) is more effective than "either/or" trade-offs.
→ Thread running through all questions: Phoenix refuses "either/or" (profit OR sustainability) and chooses "both/and". Performance zone = neither pure survival nor comfort.
Shared Value
Porter & Kramer (2011)
Creating economic AND societal value by treating social/environmental problems as innovation opportunities.
→ Q3: Café channel = shared value → normalises premium organic → economic AND societal value simultaneously.
Triple Bottom Line (TBL)
Elkington (1994)
Performance = People + Planet + Profit. Report on social and environmental impacts, not just financial.
→ Q4: Ideal framework to measure AND communicate (Scope 1/2/3, employee wellbeing). Admitting trade-offs (glass = higher transport emissions) = credibility ≠ greenwashing.
