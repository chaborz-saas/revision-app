Geopolitical Foundations and Geoeconomics

1. Geopolitics: Core Concepts

Geopolitics = study of rivalries for power/influence over a given territory (Lacoste, 2008).
  • 3 dimensions: Territory + Actors + Issue/Stake/Conflict
  • Forms of rivalry: military, economic, electoral, diplomatic
  • World = chessboard of territorial confrontations

Types of Actors

  • States — sovereign actors
  • Intra-state: parties, separatists, unions
  • Trans-state: MNCs, international institutions
  • Non-state: NGOs, lobbies, criminal orgs, consumers
Internal actors = directly in opposition on a territory.
External actors = can influence the conflict from outside (friendly/hostile relations, other interests).

3. Hard Power vs. Soft Power

Power = capacity to act on other actors to defend interests, achieve objectives, and strengthen supremacy (Verluise, 2013).
Hard PowerSoft Power
Coercive force: military, economic, technologicalNon-coercive: cultural, ideological influence
Ex: sanctions, military deployment, blockadesEx: media, education, cultural exports, diplomacy

4. Geoeconomics: Definition and Levers

Geoeconomics = use of economic instruments by States for strategic purposes (Luttwak, 1990). Economy as a tool of power, not just cooperation.
  • Bridges market logic with political objectives
  • Employed in war AND peace
  • Tied to economic sovereignty and interdependence
  • Challenges idea of self-regulating global market

Geopolitics vs. Geoeconomics

CriterionGeopoliticsGeoeconomics
FocusTerritorial controlEconomic flows
MeansForce (military)Economic instruments
ModeDirect confrontationIndirect constraint
They are complementary: distinction increasingly blurred. Contemporary states combine both logics in hybrid strategies.

Main Geoeconomic Levers

  • Sanctions & embargoes / Tariff manipulation
  • Conditional access to critical resources (rare earths)
  • Export controls on sensitive tech
  • Strategic industrial policies & FDI
  • Transport infrastructure & digital network control
  • Monetary instruments & credit diplomacy
  • Asymmetric trade agreements
  • Energy & raw material diplomacy

5. Contemporary Issues

  • Rising tensions revive geoeconomic tools
  • AI & semiconductors = central strategic levers
  • Asymmetric interdependencies = exploitable vulnerabilities
  • Need for coherent national industrial strategies
  • US-China decoupling reshaping global governance

Flash Questions

Q: What are the 3 key dimensions of geopolitical analysis?
A: Territory, Actors, Issue/Stake/Conflict.

Q: Who coined the concept of geoeconomics?
A: Edward Luttwak (1990).

Q: What is the difference between hard and soft power?
A: Hard = coercive force (military, economic); Soft = non-coercive influence (cultural, ideological).

Q: Name 3 geoeconomic levers.
A: Sanctions, tariffs, export controls, FDI, energy diplomacy, infrastructure control (any 3).

Q: What is the main focus of geoeconomics vs. geopolitics?
A: Geoeconomics = economic flows; Geopolitics = territorial control.
