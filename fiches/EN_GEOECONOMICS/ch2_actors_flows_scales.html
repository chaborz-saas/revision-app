2. Geoeconomic Arena: Types of Actors

States

  • Pursue geopolitical, geoeconomic, or hybrid strategies
  • Power = control of borders, resources, representations
  • Strategies vary by scale (local to global)
  • Authority increasingly contested

Multinational Corporations (MNCs)

  • Influence geopolitics via location, sourcing, lobbying
  • Can reinforce OR bypass State sovereignty
  • Power from control over global value chains
  • Some instrumentalized by States

Legal Non-State Actors

  • NGOs: influence agenda on HR and environment
  • International institutions: define norms structuring power
  • Think tanks & media: shape geopolitical representations

Illegal Non-State Actors

  • Organized crime (transnational), terrorism (symbolic + physical territories)
  • Can durably destabilize weak states

3. Territoriality and Scales

Territoriality is no longer limited to State borders. Networks and flows (data, capital, migrants) reshape territories. Firms' territorialization is fluid.
  • Scales: local, national, regional, global
  • Scale shapes perception of issues and definition of actors
  • Local conflicts can be instrumentalized for global purposes

4. Three Levels of Analysis

LevelFocusKey Feature
Classical GeopoliticsState struggles for territorial controlRatzel, Mackinder, Spykman. State-centric, deterministic.
Critical GeopoliticsIdentity issues, discourses, representationsQuestions narratives. Media, culture, maps as power tools.
GeoeconomicsFlows, networks, resourcesGlobalization shifts conflicts to flows. Norms = power.
Limits of classical geopolitics: State-centric, deterministic, underestimates representations/ideologies/non-state actors, poorly suited to hybrid conflicts.

5. Geographies of Power

  • Power = military + economic + normative + technological
  • Territory essential but in renewed forms
  • Power projection via networks (cables, roads, platforms)
  • Cyberspace and flows = key power dimensions
  • Supply chains = central geoeconomic issues
  • Supply disruptions used as geoeconomic tools
Integrated reading: Geopolitical + geoeconomic dynamics increasingly intertwined. Need multi-scale, multi-actor approach. Critical analysis needed to move beyond simplistic interpretations.

Flash Questions

Q: What three types of flows are at stake in the TikTok vs USA case?
A: Data flows, information flows, financial flows (ad revenue).

Q: Name the three levels of geopolitical/geoeconomic analysis.
A: Classical geopolitics, critical geopolitics, geoeconomics.

Q: What distinguishes MNCs as geoeconomic actors?
A: They control global value chains, influence geopolitics through location/sourcing/lobbying, and can reinforce or bypass State sovereignty.

Q: Why is classical geopolitics insufficient for today's conflicts?
A: It is state-centric, deterministic, ignores representations and non-state actors, and is poorly suited to hybrid conflicts.

Q: What is the Druzhba pipeline crisis about?
A: Ukraine restricted Lukoil oil transit to Central Europe, using energy flows as strategic leverage during wartime, creating tensions with Hungary and Slovakia.
