Revision Sheet — Geoeconomics Ch 3

The Company as a Geoeconomic Actor

1. Companies and Geopolitics

TNCs are not mere economic agents: they influence geopolitics and operate in high-risk areas where States are not always predominant (Kobrin, 2015; De Villa et al., 2015).
  • Multipolar world = complex strategic trade-offs
  • Norm/sanction conflicts reshape value chains
  • Political instability influences location and investment
  • Firms manage transnational, fluctuating geopolitical risks

Company as Normative Actor

  • Some MNCs influence international rules (digital, ecological standards)
  • Normative power depends on size, sector, lobbying capacity

2. Global Value Chains & Interdependencies

  • Companies structure interconnected global production networks
  • These generate interdependencies AND vulnerabilities
  • Must consider resilience of value chains
  • Geopolitical risks = highly unpredictable and intense
Key risk: Concentration of production in a single country = single point of failure exploitable by rivals. Asymmetric interdependencies make some actors more vulnerable.

3. Strategies for Managing Geopolitical Risk

StrategyDescription
Scenario planningMap geopolitical risks, anticipate disruptions
Contractual mechanismsForce majeure clauses, risk-sharing provisions
Geographic diversificationDiversify production/supply across multiple regions
Enhanced dialogueEngage with governments and local institutions

4. Geoeconomic Diagnostic: 6 Steps

  1. Characterize the company: mission, vision, values, production, supply, sales territories
  2. Analyze stakeholders: mutual dependence, stability, strategic importance
  3. Analyze areas of activity: crises/tensions affecting production or sales, map actors
  4. Analyze activity flows: crises affecting supply or sales flows, actors involved
  5. Visual synthesis: map/diagram showing territories, actors, flows, power levers
  6. Evaluate data reliability: source, author, date, biases, external validations, expert

Stakeholder Analysis Checklist

  • Mission, vision, values, history
  • Company's dependence on stakeholder (% supply/sales)
  • Stakeholder's dependence on company
  • Stakeholder stability (financial, political, reputational)

5. Data Reliability: 6-Step Evaluation

Mnemonic - SADBE²:
Source (reliable?), Author (credible?), Date (up to date?), Biases (our own?), External validations (triangulation?), Expert (accessible?).

Flash Questions

Q: What makes TNCs geoeconomic actors?
A: They influence geopolitics through location/sourcing/lobbying, their decisions affect State power relations, and they operate in high-risk areas.

Q: Name the 4 strategies for managing geopolitical risk.
A: Scenario planning, contractual mechanisms, geographic diversification, enhanced dialogue with governments.

Q: What are the 6 steps of a geoeconomic diagnostic?
A: Characterize company, analyze stakeholders, analyze areas of activity, analyze flows, visual synthesis, evaluate data reliability.

Q: What are the 6 criteria for data reliability?
A: Source, author, date, our biases, external validations, accessible expert.

Q: What does asymmetric interdependency mean for value chains?
A: Some actors in a chain are more vulnerable to disruption than others, creating exploitable geoeconomic leverage.
