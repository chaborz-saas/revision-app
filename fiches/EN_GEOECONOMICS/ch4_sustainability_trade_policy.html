Revision Sheet — Geoeconomics Ch 4

Sustainability, Trade Policy, and Geoeconomics

1. Companies as Responsible Geoeconomic Actors

Environment & Geopolitical Risk

  • Climate change = "threat multiplier" for resource tensions
  • Natural disasters disrupt economic activity
  • Companies must integrate climate resilience into strategy
  • Rising challenges of energy sovereignty

Social Responsibility & Territorial Acceptability

Territorial acceptability = degree to which a company's presence is accepted by local community and stakeholders. Without it: protests, regulatory obstacles, reputational damage.
  • Must integrate local/ethical issues
  • Expected to contribute to sustainable development
  • Requires understanding power relations and local identities

MNCs in International Cooperation

  • Can engage in multilateral processes, conflict resolution, economic diplomacy
  • Bridging role between public and private actors
  • Commitments sometimes criticized as instrumentalization
  • Strengthened dialogue with NGOs and intl. organizations

Gray Zones

Gray zones = areas with weak State governance. Risks: corruption, violence, exploitation. Companies must adopt robust ethical policies and combine diplomacy, ethics, and performance.

2. Free Trade vs. Protectionism

Trade policies = instruments through which governments influence international trade flows. Two types: free trade and protectionist.
Free Trade (Pros)Free Trade (Cons)
Efficiency via comparative advantageSectoral losses in open economies
Fosters innovation (competition)Exacerbates social inequalities
Lower prices, wider choiceExcessive dependence on foreign suppliers
Overall wealth growthIgnores environmental/social externalities

Justifications for Protectionism

  • Infant industry: temporarily protect developing sector
  • Strategic trade policy: support national champion in oligopolistic sector
  • Unfair practices: countermeasures vs. subsidies/dumping
  • Non-economic: sovereignty, security, ecological transition

Ambiguous Effects of Protectionism

  • May cause welfare losses
  • Market distortions, reduced innovation incentives (debated)
  • Risk of trade retaliation
  • Targeted protectionism can support industrial strategy
Contemporary trend: Revived interest in targeted protectionism (reshoring). Debates: full free trade vs. strategic regionalism. Trade policies = choices between efficiency, equity, sovereignty.

Flash Questions

Q: What is territorial acceptability?
A: The degree to which a company's presence is accepted by local community and stakeholders.

Q: Name 4 justifications for protectionism.
A: Infant industry, strategic trade policy, response to unfair practices, non-economic objectives (sovereignty, security).

Q: What are gray zones in geoeconomics?
A: Areas with weak State governance where companies face risks of corruption, violence, and exploitation.

Q: What are the 4 SDGs to focus on for the exam?
A: SDG 2 (Zero Hunger), SDG 6 (Clean Water), SDG 7 (Clean Energy), SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities).

Q: What is the HOS model?
A: Heckscher-Ohlin-Samuelson model: countries should specialize according to their abundant factors of production (theoretical basis for free trade).

Q: What is the trade policy dilemma?
A: Complex political choice between efficiency, equity, and sovereignty. No universally optimal policy.
