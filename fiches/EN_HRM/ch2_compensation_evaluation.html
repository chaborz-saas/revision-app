Revision Sheet — HRM Ch 2

Compensation & Evaluation

1. Four Elements of Compensation

ElementKey Points
1. Direct PayBasic (fixed) + Performance (variable) + Bonuses (role-linked)
2. Statutory BenefitsMandatory by law: Health, Pension, Leave, Unemployment, CPF, Severance...
3. In-Kind BenefitsIndividual (car, housing, phone) + Collective (meal vouchers, leisure, loans)
4. Deferred CompensationProfit-sharing, Mandatory participation, Savings plans, Stock options

2. Deferred Compensation Details

MechanismKey Rules
Profit-sharing (voluntary)1959; max 20% gross pay; optional; 20% social tax
Mandatory participation1967; 50+ employees; net profit > 5% of equity; special reserve
Company Savings Plan (PEE)1967; locked 5 years; employer top-up possible; 20% tax
Stock Options1970/1983; buy at pre-set price; mainly for executives

3. Payroll Calculation

Formula:
Net + 25% = Gross
Gross + 42% employer contribution = Payroll cost
Example: 10 managers at 4,000 net/month → Gross = 5,000/month → Annual = 60,000 → + 42% = 85,200/person → Total = 852,000/year

4. Fixed-Term Contracts (CDD)

  • Written contract required
  • Trial: 1 day/week worked; max 2 weeks (<6 mo) or 1 month (>6 mo)
  • Renewal: max 2 times
  • Waiting period: 1/3 of duration if >14 days, 1/2 otherwise
  • Severance: 10% of gross (e.g., 3 mo x 2,000 gross = 600 euros)

5. Three Wage Policies (Peretti)

  • Cost competitiveness: Reduce fixed, increase variable pay
  • Quality competitiveness: Reward skills and qualifications; individualized pay
  • Financial market: Align with long-term results; more deferred pay

6. Four Qualities of Good Compensation

COMPETITIVE (match industry) + FLEXIBLE (adapt to change) + FAIR (equitable) + MOTIVATING (reward effort)

7. Performance Evaluation

Definition: A continuous and strategic process of assessing, measuring, and developing individual/team performance in alignment with organizational goals.

Objectives

For the CompanyFor the Employee
Understand employees betterShare work situation
Improve team managementExpress management preferences
Suggest improvement actionsShare goals & ambitions
Organize performance infoGet feedback; learn about opportunities

What to evaluate?

  • Performance: Achievement of objectives
  • Behavior: Communication, collaboration
  • Potential: Growth capacity (succession planning)

8. Evaluation Tools

  • B.O.S. — Behavioral Observation Scales
  • B.A.R.S. — Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales (reduces subjectivity)
  • F.D.R.S. — Forced Distribution Ranking Systems
  • SMART Goals — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound
  • 360-degree — Feedback from multiple sources
  • Annual appraisal interview — Formal yearly review
  • Career development interview — Mandatory every 2 years since 2014

9. Criticisms of Evaluation

Unfair/imprecise • Administrative burden • Contradictory goals (control vs. develop) • No real performance boost • Demotivating

10. Compensation Allocation Principles

Bonuses = reward achieving objectives
Salary increases = reward efforts, motivate continued effort
Fairness: Same bonus for same objective; equal pay for equal skills regardless of seniority

Flash Questions

Q: What are the 4 elements of compensation?
A: Direct pay, Statutory benefits, In-kind benefits, Deferred compensation
Q: At what threshold is mandatory profit-sharing (participation) required?
A: Companies with 50+ employees and net profits exceeding 5% of equity
Q: What are the 4 qualities of a good compensation policy?
A: Competitive, Flexible, Fair, Motivating
Q: What does B.A.R.S. stand for and what does it reduce?
A: Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales; reduces subjectivity and leniency in evaluation
Q: How often is the career development interview mandatory?
A: At least every 2 years, mandatory since 2014
