Revision Sheet — Ch 3: CVP Analysis

Performance Management • Key formulas, marginal analysis & flash questions

Cost Behaviour

TypeBehaviourExample
FixedConstant regardless of volumeRent
VariableChanges proportionally with volumeRaw materials
Semi-variableFixed component + variable componentElectricity
High-Low Method:
Variable cost/unit = (High cost − Low cost) / (High volume − Low volume)
Fixed cost = Total cost − (Variable cost/unit × Volume)

Key CVP Formulas

Contribution per unit = Selling price − Variable cost per unit
Contribution margin % = Contribution / Revenue × 100
BEP (units) = Fixed Costs / Contribution per unit
Margin of Safety (units) = Planned volume − BEP volume
Margin of Safety (%) = (Planned − BEP) / Planned × 100
Target Profit Volume = (Fixed Costs + Target Profit) / Contribution per unit

Operating Gearing

Higher proportion of fixed costs = higher operating gearing = greater sensitivity of profit to volume changes (amplifies gains AND losses).

Weaknesses of BEP Analysis

  • Assumes linear cost/revenue/volume relationships
  • Stepped fixed costs not accounted for
  • Fixed costs not truly fixed at all activity levels
  • Multi-product allocation of fixed costs is problematic

Marginal Analysis — 4 Decision Types

1. Assessing Contracts

Accept if contract revenue > relevant variable costs (positive contribution). Fixed costs ignored if unchanged.

2. Scarce Resources (Limiting Factor)

3 steps: (1) Confirm resource is scarce → (2) Contribution per unit of scarce resource → (3) Rank & allocate from highest.

3. Make or Buy

Spare capacity: compare variable cost vs buy price.
No spare capacity: add opportunity cost to variable cost, then compare.

4. Close / Continue

Do NOT close a department making a positive contribution. If fixed costs stay, closing loses the contribution.

Flash Questions

Q: Fixed costs = £500. Contribution = £2/unit. What is the BEP?
A: 250 units (£500 / £2).
Q: Planned sales = 500 units, BEP = 250 units. What is the margin of safety?
A: 250 units = 50% (250/500 × 100).
Q: Fixed costs = £3,000, contribution = £7/unit. Target profit = £4,000. Volume needed?
A: 1,000 units ((£3,000 + £4,000) / £7).
Q: Machine hours limited. B17 gives £4/hr, B14 gives £3.75/hr. Which to produce first?
A: B17 — highest contribution per unit of scarce resource.
Q: A department shows a £9,000 loss after fixed cost allocation but contributes £37,000. Should it close?
A: No — closing would reduce total profit by £37,000 (fixed costs stay).
Q: Variable cost to make = £15, opportunity cost = £12, buy price = £20. Make or buy?
A: Buy. Making costs £27 (£15 + £12) vs buying at £20.
