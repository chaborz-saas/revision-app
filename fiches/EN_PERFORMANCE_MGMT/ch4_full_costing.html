Revision Sheet — Ch 4: Full Costing

Performance Management • Key formulas, overhead recovery & flash questions

Full (Absorption) Cost

Full cost = Total resources sacrificed = Variable costs + Fixed costs (all direct + indirect).
  • Uses: Pricing, control, efficiency comparison, performance assessment

Process Costing (Single Product)

Cost per unit = Total cost / Units produced
Equivalent units = Completed + (WIP × % complete)
Cost per equiv. unit = Total cost / Equivalent units

Job Costing (Multi Product)

Direct cost: Traceable to a specific job (materials, labour on that job).
Indirect cost (overhead): Cannot be traced to a specific job (rent, depreciation).
Full cost = Direct cost + Overhead absorbed
Direct/indirect is NOT the same as variable/fixed. A cost can be direct and fixed, or indirect and variable. It depends on traceability to the cost unit.

Overhead Recovery Rates

Overhead recovery rate = Total overheads / Total basis units
Common bases: Direct labour hours (labour-intensive) | Machine hours (capital-intensive) | % of direct labour cost

Cost Centre Overhead Steps

  1. Allocate — assign specific overheads to their cost centre
  2. Apportion — share common overheads using a fair basis
  3. Reapportion — distribute service centre costs to production centres
  4. Calculate overhead recovery rate per production centre
  5. Absorb overheads into jobs based on usage

Absorption vs Variable Costing

AbsorptionVariable (Marginal)
Product costAll production costsVariable costs only
Fixed mfg OHIn inventoryPeriod expense
StatementGross profitContribution margin
External reportingRequired (IAS 2)Not compliant
Profit difference rule:
Inventory increases → Absorption profit > Variable profit
Inventory decreases → Absorption profit < Variable profit
Difference = Δ inventory units × Fixed OH per unit
Reconciliation: Variable profit + Fixed OH in closing stock − Fixed OH in opening stock = Absorption profit

Flash Questions

Q: Total cost = £5,280, equivalent units = 132. Cost per unit?
A: £40 (£5,280 / 132).
Q: Overheads = £10,000, direct labour hours = 1,000. A job uses 3 hours. Overhead charged?
A: £30 (rate = £10/hr × 3 hrs).
Q: Production = 6,000, sales = 4,000. Fixed OH = £3/unit. Which method shows higher profit?
A: Absorption costing (inventory increased, so absorption profit is higher by 2,000 × £3 = £6,000).
Q: What is the difference between allocating and apportioning overheads?
A: Allocating = assigning costs specific to one centre. Apportioning = sharing costs across multiple centres using a fair basis.
Q: Is direct cost always variable?
A: No. Direct/indirect relates to traceability; variable/fixed relates to behaviour with volume. They are independent.
Q: What is a service cost centre?
A: A support department (e.g. HR, maintenance) whose costs are reapportioned to production cost centres.
