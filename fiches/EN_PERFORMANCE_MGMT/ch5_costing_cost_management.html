Performance Management • Key concepts, formulas & flash questions

Traditional vs. Modern Environment

AspectTraditionalModern
ProductionLabour-intensive, labour-pacedCapital-intensive, machine-paced
OverheadsLow relative to direct costsHigh relative to direct costs
MarketRelatively uncompetitiveHighly competitive, international
Product lifeLong cyclesShort cycles, design stage critical

Activity-Based Costing (ABC)

  • Treats overheads as driven by activities, not just volume
  • Collects overhead costs into cost pools based on cost drivers
  • 6 steps: Identify activities → costs → drivers → pools → allocate → charge to products
Cost/driver unit = Total cost pool ÷ Total driver units
Overhead/product = Cost/driver unit × Drivers used by product
Key: ABC is most useful when overheads are high and products consume activities differently. Best suited for large businesses in competitive markets.

Total Life-Cycle Costing

  • Three phases: Pre-production (R&D, design, setup) | Production (make & sell) | Post-production (after-sales, closure)
  • High proportion of costs committed in the pre-production phase

Target Costing

Target Cost = Target Price − Target Profit
  • Market-based approach, used in pre-production phase
  • Steps: Set target price → target profit → target cost → estimate cost → compare → reduce if needed

Kaizen Costing

  • Continuous, gradual cost reduction in production phase
  • Sets periodic cost reduction targets, compares with actuals
  • Focuses on eliminating waste: overproduction, defects, bottlenecks, excess inventory, waiting times
Target costing = pre-production | Kaizen costing = production | Life-cycle costing = all phases

Value Chain Analysis

  • Primary: Inbound logistics → Operations → Outbound logistics → Marketing/Sales → Service
  • Support: HR, Technology/R&D, Procurement, Admin/Finance/QA
  • Eliminate non-value-adding activities; configure for cost advantage or differentiation

Benchmarking

  • Compare with best-in-class operation (internal or external)
  • 6 steps: Choose function → find benchmark → select measures → collect data → identify gaps → implement improvements
  • Competitor benchmarking = best but competitor may not share data

Total Quality Management (TQM)

Cost TypePhaseExamples
PreventionBefore productionTraining, equipment, process design
AppraisalDuring productionInspection, testing, quality audits
Internal failureBefore deliveryRework, scrap, re-testing
External failureAfter deliveryComplaints, returns, recalls, reputation loss
More prevention spending → less appraisal + failure costs. Goal = zero defects.

Flash Questions

Q: What does ABC stand for and what does it improve?
Activity-Based Costing — provides more accurate overhead allocation to products.
Q: Formula for target cost?
Target Cost = Target Price − Target Profit.
Q: When is kaizen costing used vs. target costing?
Kaizen = production phase (gradual reduction); Target = pre-production phase (cost ceiling).
Q: Name the four TQM cost categories.
Prevention, appraisal, internal failure, external failure.
Q: What are cost drivers in ABC?
Factors that cause a change in the costs of a support activity (e.g., number of setups, machine hours).
Q: What is the key insight about prevention costs in TQM?
Investing more in prevention reduces appraisal and failure costs.
