Revision Sheet — Ch 6: Budgeting

Performance Management • Key concepts, formulas & flash questions

Definitions

  • Budget: Short-term business plan (usually 1 year), expressed in financial/physical terms
  • Forecast: Prediction of future environment — informs budgets but is not a plan
  • Limiting factor: Constraint that restricts achievement of objectives (usually sales demand)

5 Uses of Budgets

  1. Promote forward thinking
  2. Coordinate business activities
  3. Motivate performance
  4. Provide basis for control (variances)
  5. Provide system of authorisation

Budget Types

TypeDescription
PeriodicFixed period (usually 1 year)
Rolling (continual)Continuously updated to always cover a full planning period
IncrementalPrevious period + adjustments
Zero-base (ZBB)Start from zero; all spending must be justified
Activity-based (ABB)Based on cost-driving activities, extends ABC principles

Key Formulas

Purchases = COGS + Closing inventory − Opening inventory
Closing cash = Opening cash + Receipts − Payments
Closing receivables = Opening + Credit sales − Cash received
Closing payables = Opening + Credit purchases − Cash paid

Cash Budget Rules

  • Depreciation is NOT a cash flow — exclude from cash budget
  • Credit terms create timing lags (e.g., 1-month credit means Jan sales → Feb cash)
  • Inventory changes affect purchases: build inventory = buy more; reduce = buy less
  • Quarterly payments (e.g., electricity) only appear in the quarter they are paid

Master Budget Structure

  • Sales budget (start here if sales = limiting factor)
  • Production budget = Sales + Closing FG inventory − Opening FG inventory
  • Materials, labour, overheads budgets
  • Trade receivables & payables budgets
  • Cash budget (synthesis of all cash flows)
  • Budgeted income statement & statement of financial position

ABB Steps

  1. Set sales budget
  2. Identify activities needed
  3. Identify cost drivers
  4. Calculate cost driver rate
  5. Estimate total driver usage
  6. Activity budget = Total driver usage × Driver rate

Flash Questions

Q: Difference between a budget and a forecast?
Budget = plan with intention to achieve; Forecast = prediction of the environment.
Q: Why start with the limiting factor?
All other budgets depend on it; it sets the ceiling for what the business can achieve.
Q: Why exclude depreciation from the cash budget?
Depreciation is a non-cash expense — it does not involve any cash payment.
Q: What is zero-base budgeting?
All spending starts from zero and must be justified; prevents perpetuating past inefficiencies.
Q: What is ABB?
Activity-Based Budgeting: budgets based on cost-driving activities (extends ABC to budgeting).
Q: Name 3 features of Beyond Budgeting.
Rolling forecasts, decentralised decision-making, rewards linked to performance improvement.
