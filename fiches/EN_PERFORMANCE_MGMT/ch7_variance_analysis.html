Revision Sheet — Ch 7: Standard Costing & Variance Analysis

Performance Management • All variance formulas & flash questions

Flexed Budgets

Flexed figure = Original budget × (Actual output / Budgeted output)
Variable items are flexed. Fixed costs stay the same.

All Variance Formulas

Sales Variances

Sales Volume Variance = Flexed budget profit − Original budget profit
F if sold more; A if sold fewer
Sales Price Variance = Actual revenue − (Actual units × Std price)
F if sold at higher price; A if sold at lower price

Direct Materials Variances

Usage Variance = (Std qty for actual output − Actual qty) × Std price
F if used less; A if used more
Price Variance = (Actual qty × Std price) − Actual cost
F if paid less; A if paid more

Direct Labour Variances

Efficiency Variance = (Std hours for actual output − Actual hours) × Std rate
F if worked faster; A if worked slower
Rate Variance = (Actual hours × Std rate) − Actual labour cost
F if paid less per hour; A if paid more

Fixed Overhead Variance

Spending Variance = Budgeted fixed OH − Actual fixed OH
F if spent less; A if spent more. NOT flexed.

Profit Reconciliation

Actual Profit = Budget Profit + Favourable variances − Adverse variances

Causes of Variances

VarianceCommon Causes
Sales volume (A)Poor sales staff, market decline, production shortage
Sales price (A)Price competition, poor negotiation
Materials usage (A)Scrap/waste, substandard materials, faulty machinery
Materials price (A)Poor buying, higher quality, market price rise
Labour efficiency (A)Poor supervision, low skill, machine problems
Labour rate (A)Higher grade workers, pay rises, overtime
Fixed OH (A)Poor control, unexpected cost increases

Standard Costing

  • Standards = budgeted quantities & values for one unit
  • Ideal standards: perfect conditions (rarely achievable)
  • Practical standards: achievable but challenging
  • Used for: performance evaluation, pricing, inventory costing, sourcing decisions
Problems: Can become outdated quickly; manager accountability issues; no incentive beyond standard; can create perverse behaviour (e.g., excess inventory).

Flash Questions

Q: Why flex the budget?
To make it comparable to actual output — you cannot compare a budget for 1,000 units with actuals for 900.
Q: Materials usage variance formula?
(Std qty for actual output − Actual qty) × Std price. Favourable if used less.
Q: Is fixed overhead flexed?
No — fixed costs do not change with volume. Variance = Budget OH − Actual OH.
Q: What are compensating variances?
Linked favourable and adverse variances (e.g., lower price → higher volume) that partially offset.
Q: Profit reconciliation format?
Budget profit + Favourable variances − Adverse variances = Actual profit.
Q: Ideal vs practical standards?
Ideal = perfect conditions; Practical = achievable but challenging (better for motivation).
