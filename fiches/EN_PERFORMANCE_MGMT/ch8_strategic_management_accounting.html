Revision Sheet — Ch 8: Strategic Management Accounting

Performance Management • SMA, EVA, Pricing, Divisions & Transfer Pricing

SMA Overview

  • Outward-looking, focused on outperforming competition
  • Provides information for strategic plans and decisions
  • Covers: competitor analysis, customer profitability, balanced scorecard, EVA, pricing

Competitor Analysis — 4 Aspects

  1. Mission & objectives — profit, growth, market share goals
  2. Strategies — tech investments, alliances, M&A, cost reduction
  3. Assumptions — beliefs about industry trends, strengths
  4. Resources & capabilities — scale, technology, cost structure, reputation

Customer Profitability Analysis (CPA)

  • Assess profitability per customer/type; not all high-revenue customers are profitable
  • Costs: order handling, visits, delivery, inventory holding, credit, after-sales
  • Use trend analysis to spot recurring vs. one-off issues

Balanced Scorecard (4 Perspectives)

PerspectiveExamples
FinancialROCE, profit margin, revenue growth
CustomerSatisfaction, new customer growth
Internal Processes% new product sales, cycle times, complaint response
Learning & GrowthEmployee skills, motivation, IS capabilities
Cause-and-effect: Learning → Better processes → Happier customers → Better financials

EVA (Economic Value Added)

EVA = NOPAT − (R × C)
NOPAT = Adjusted net operating profit after tax
R = Required rate of return (cost of capital)
C = Capital invested (net assets)
Adjustments to NOPAT: Add back capitalised marketing (unexpired portion), excess allowances, restructuring costs. Exclude investment income. Apply tax rate.

Pricing

MethodKey Point
Full cost-plusFull cost + mark-up; useful benchmark but market may not accept
Marginal cost-plusVariable cost + mark-up; OK short-term, must cover all costs long-term
Target pricingMarket sets price; costs must fit within target
PenetrationLow initial price to gain market share; raise later
Price skimmingHigh initial price for insensitive buyers; lower gradually
Profit maximised where Marginal Revenue = Marginal Cost

ROI & Residual Income

ROI = Divisional Profit / Divisional Investment × 100%
RI = Divisional Profit − (Investment × Cost of Capital)
ROI problem: A division may reject a good project (above cost of capital) because it would lower the division's existing ROI. RI avoids this problem.

Transfer Pricing

MethodBest When
Market priceExternal market exists (usually best = opportunity cost)
Variable costSelling division has spare capacity
Full cost + loadingNo market exists; but passes inefficiencies
NegotiatedPromotes independence; can be unfair
Ideal transfer price = opportunity cost. Spare capacity → min price = variable cost. Full capacity → min price = market price.

Flash Questions

Q: What is the EVA formula?
EVA = NOPAT − (R × C). Positive EVA = value created above required return.
Q: Name the 4 balanced scorecard perspectives.
Financial, Customer, Internal Business Processes, Learning & Growth.
Q: Difference between penetration pricing and price skimming?
Penetration = low price first to gain share; Skimming = high price first for insensitive buyers, lower gradually.
Q: Why might ROI lead to bad decisions?
A division may reject projects above cost of capital because they would lower the division's existing ROI.
Q: What is the ideal basis for transfer pricing?
Opportunity cost for both divisions. Market price is usually the best proxy.
Q: What is CPA?
Customer Profitability Analysis — assesses profitability per customer including all selling/distribution costs.
Q: 3 types of responsibility centres?
Cost centre (costs), Profit centre (revenues + costs), Investment centre (revenues + costs + investment).
