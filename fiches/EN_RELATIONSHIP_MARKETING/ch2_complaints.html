Relationship Marketing — Chapter 2

Complaints — Last Chance Relationships

1. Cost of Dissatisfaction
Direct costs: Warranties, indemnities, complaint handling costs
Opportunity costs: Lost revenue from defecting customers + negative word-of-mouth deterring prospects
Total cost of dissatisfaction = Direct costs + Opportunity costs. Example: a single B2B complaint can cost ~950 EUR.
2. Complaints Management Process (5 Steps)
#StepGoal
1Collection & CentralizationGather all complaints into one system
2Analysis & ValidationInvestigate, determine if justified
3Providing ResponseCommunicate solution to customer
4Corrective ActionFix the specific customer's problem
5Progress ActionsSystemic changes to prevent recurrence
3. Compensation Grid
Principle: Standard grid matching customer type (strategic/important/standard) x criticality (low/medium/high) to appropriate compensation level.
Built jointly by Sales + Finance + Quality. Saves time, avoids favoritism, ensures consistency.
4. Corrective vs. Preventive Actions
Corrective: Fix the individual case (exchange, refund, discount)
Preventive: Remove root causes. May require rethinking organization, processes, suppliers, skills.
Ishikawa / Fishbone Diagram: Root-cause analysis tool. 6 Ms = Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Milieu (Environment)
Look for the cause of the cause of the cause! Surface fixes are insufficient.
5. Complaints Database & KPIs
Database fields: ID, date, customer, product, complaint object, journey stage, customer action, verbatim, cause, corrective action
  • KPI 1: Evolution of complaint numbers over time
  • KPI 2: Types of complaints
  • KPI 3: Costs of complaints
  • KPI 4: Treatment effectiveness (customer feelings)
  • KPI 5: Response time (avg. duration, % within target)
6. Pareto Analysis
80/20 rule: ~20% of cause types generate ~80% of complaints. Focus prevention on the most frequent issues.
Visual, easy to implement. Forces quantitative thinking. Not a scientific law but a powerful heuristic.
7. Key Formula
Attrition Rate = Customers lost during period / Customers at start of period
~95% of dissatisfied customers do NOT complain. Complaints are the tip of the iceberg. Many leave silently.
8. Flash Questions
What are the 5 steps of complaints management?
1) Collection & Centralization 2) Analysis & Validation 3) Providing Response 4) Corrective Action 5) Progress/Preventive Actions
What is the Ishikawa Diagram used for?
Root-cause analysis using 6 Ms: Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Milieu. Finds the cause of the cause of the cause.
What is Pareto's rule applied to complaints?
~20% of complaint types cause ~80% of all complaints. Focus preventive actions on the most frequent causes.
What percentage of dissatisfied customers typically complain?
Only about 5%. The vast majority (95%) leave without expressing their dissatisfaction.
What 3 departments should build the compensation grid?
Sales, Finance, and Quality departments collaboratively.
