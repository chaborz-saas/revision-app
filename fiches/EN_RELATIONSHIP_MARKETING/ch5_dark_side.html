Relationship Marketing — Chapter 5

The Dark Side of Relationship Marketing

1. Five Pitfalls to Avoid
#Pitfall (FALSE belief)Reality
1CRM = IT systemsTech is a support to strategy. Marketing department must drive what CRM does.
2RM compensates for poor qualityRM = icing on the cake. Without quality product (the cake), RM is useless.
3RM is top-down (brand to customer)Must be bidirectional. Listen to customers! Social media amplifies this need.
4RM goal = immediate salesBalance short-term promos with long-term emotional/cognitive connections. Promos alone = sales drop when they stop.
5RM is a stable activityRM evolves constantly with data, tech, regulations, and consumer behavior.
2. Pitfall Details
Pitfall 1: Buying Salesforce does NOT mean you are doing CRM. Strategy comes FIRST, then IT implements.
Pitfall 2: Consumers are irritated by solicitation when the basic contract is not fulfilled. Quality first!
Pitfall 3: "Don't just talk to customers -- learn from them!" Policies based only on control don't work.
Pitfall 4: Promotional campaigns = short-term spike. Relationship programs = sustained increasing income over time.
Pitfall 5: Data field is constantly evolving. Strategies must be continuously adapted.
3. Limits: Ethical Issues
Unfair practices: Which RM practices cross ethical lines? What questions does AI in RM raise?
Not universal: RM is not equally beneficial for all companies, industries, or product types.
4. Limits: Consumer Perceptions
  • Intrusion: Over-solicitation, feeling invaded
  • Privacy: Discomfort with data collection
  • Manipulation: Personalization used to influence decisions
  • Inauthenticity: "Relationships" perceived as insincere, profit-driven
5. Consumer Gaming Strategies
Cherry-picking: Only buying during promotions
Program hopping: Joining many programs without loyalty
Gaming rewards: Exploiting rules for max rewards, min spending
Serial switching: Constantly capturing new-customer offers
6. The Personalization Paradox & GDPR
Consumers want personalized experiences BUT are uncomfortable with the data collection required. This is the personalization paradox.
GDPR perception: Consumers do not perceive GDPR as effective protection. Consent forms are confusing, little real control, companies still collect data.
7. Flash Questions
What are the 5 common pitfalls of Relationship Marketing?
1) CRM = IT 2) RM compensates poor quality 3) RM is top-down 4) RM = immediate sales 5) RM is stable. All are FALSE.
Why is "RM = IT" a pitfall?
Technology is only a support to strategy. Marketing must define objectives and actions first. Buying CRM software alone is not doing CRM.
What is the personalization paradox?
Consumers want personalized experiences but are uncomfortable with the data collection needed to deliver them.
What is the "cake analogy" for Pitfall 2?
Basic quality = the cake. RM = the icing. Without a good cake (quality product), the icing (RM) is useless and even irritating.
Name 3 consumer gaming strategies against RM policies.
Cherry-picking (only buying promos), program hopping (joining many without loyalty), serial switching (capturing new-customer offers).
