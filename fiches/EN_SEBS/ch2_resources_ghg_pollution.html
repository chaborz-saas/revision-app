Revision Sheet — Ch 2: Mineral Resources, GHG & Pollution

1. Mineral Resources

Key Facts
  • Human-made products now outweigh living organisms on Earth
  • Same upward extraction trend for all resources, including metals
  • Even sand is under pressure (construction, electronics)
  • Critical metals (rare earths, cobalt, lithium) essential for energy transition
  • Geopolitical concentration: China (rare earths), DRC (cobalt)

Flash Q: Why are critical metals a concern for the energy transition?
Essential for batteries, solar panels, wind turbines, EVs — but concentrated geographically and have very low recycling rates.

2. Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Emissions

Greenhouse Effect Mechanism
  1. Sun's radiation partially reaches Earth
  2. Earth heats up and emits radiation back
  3. GHGs trap this radiation, warming the planet

Without GHG effect: -18 degrees C (frozen). Natural effect: ~15 degrees C. Human-enhanced: potentially +20 degrees C.

Critical numbers: Current warming speed = ~5 degrees C/century vs natural 0.05 degrees C/century = nearly 100x faster than natural changes. Already +1.4 degrees C observed.
Flash Q: What are the IPCC's 3 key messages?
(1) Humans cause accelerated climate change. (2) Limit to +2 degrees C. (3) Beyond that = risk of runaway warming.

3. Other Pollutions

  • Eutrophication: Nutrient excess (N, P) in water = algal blooms, oxygen depletion
  • Health impact: 90% of pollution-related deaths in developing countries
  • Plastic: Never fully degrades, rivers carry it to oceans, microplastic contamination
  • Water quality: Deteriorating globally, affecting billions

4. Biodiversity & Living Resources

  • 6th mass extinction underway — clear acceleration
  • Drivers: habitat destruction, overexploitation, pollution, invasive species, climate change
  • Food system = major GHG contributor (beef has highest carbon footprint)

Flash Q: What is the Jevons Paradox?
When efficiency increases, resource use may actually increase (not decrease) because the resource becomes cheaper/more attractive. Also called the "rebound effect."

6. The Circular Economy

Definition & Principles
  • Definition: Holistic approach eliminating waste by keeping resources in circulation
  • Opposite of linear "Extract, Manufacture, Use, Dispose"
  • Mindset: mass consumption → optimal use
3 Principles
  • Reduce: Minimize resources/waste, design for efficiency & durability
  • Reuse: Repair, refurbish, repurpose, sharing programs
  • Recycle: Convert waste to resources, recyclable & disassemblable materials
SD is not limited to environmental issues. It is systemic. The circular economy is the framework for transforming linear "processing" into a regenerative model.
