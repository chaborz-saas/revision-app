Summary — HRM Ch 2: Compensation & Evaluation

1. Elements of Compensation

Compensation in HRM comprises four main categories: direct pay, statutory benefits, in-kind benefits, and deferred compensation.

1.1 Direct Pay

  • Basic pay (fixed): Base pay + personal supplement
  • Performance pay (variable): Based on individual and/or team results
  • Fixed and variable bonuses: Not related to individual results; mostly linked to job role, duties, or position

1.2 Statutory Benefits

Statutory Benefits: Mandatory benefits that employers are legally required to provide under national labor laws or employment regulations. They form the foundation of employee protection and vary by country.

In France, statutory benefits include:

  • Health Insurance
  • Sick Leave
  • Pension Contributions
  • Maternity/Paternity Leave
  • Paid Annual Leave
  • Work Accident Insurance
  • Unemployment Insurance
  • Family Benefits
  • Training Rights (CPF)
  • Severance Pay

1.4 Deferred Compensation / Peripheral Benefits

1.4.1 Profit-Sharing (Voluntary)

  • Created in 1959, revised in 1986
  • Company distributes part of its profit to employees, up to a maximum of 20% of gross pay
  • Optional scheme covering all employees
  • Payment subject to social contribution (20% since 2012)

1.4.2 Mandatory Profit-Sharing (Participation)

  • Created in 1967
  • Mandatory for companies with 50+ employees where net profits exceed 5% of equity
  • Amounts can be paid immediately or saved, at the employee's discretion
  • Subject to 20% tax
  • The portion of profits is called the special profit-sharing reserve

1.4.3 Company Savings Plan (PEE)

  • Created in 1967; a collective savings system for employees
  • Employees can deposit profit-sharing and incentive bonuses
  • Employees can also make extra contributions
  • Company may add matching contributions (employer top-up)
  • Participation is optional
  • Deposited amounts are locked for 5 years (except authorized early release)
  • 20% social contribution rate on locked amounts

1.4.4 Stock Options

  • Governed by 1970 & 1983 laws
  • Employees can buy shares at a pre-set price after a specified period (usually 5–10 years)
  • Mainly allocated to executives and managers

1.5 Contractual Benefits

Social benefits to employees through agreements, structured by time horizon:

  • Short-term: Health insurance, car insurance (immediate protection)
  • Medium-term: Time-saving account (work-life balance)
  • Long-term: Pension supplements, disability pensions, access to retirement homes, life insurance

2. Compensation Management

2.1 Payroll

Payroll: The total amount of money a company pays its employees before taxes and other deductions, including both employees' gross wages and employer's contributions for social charges and benefits.
Payroll Calculation Formula:
Net wage + social contribution (~25%) = Gross wage
Gross wage + employer contribution (~42%) = Payroll cost
Example: 10 managers earning 4,000 euros net/month:
• Gross = 4,000 x 1.25 = 5,000/month = 60,000/year per manager
• Employer cost = 60,000 x 0.42 = 25,200
• Total per manager = 85,200/year
• Total payroll = 85,200 x 10 = 852,000 euros/year

2.2 Factors Affecting Payroll

Employment trends:

  • Employee movement (joining and resignation)
  • Internal promotions
  • Flexibility options (overtime, fixed-term contracts, temporary work, outsourcing)

Compensation trends:

  • General wage hike
  • Category-specific wage hike
  • Individual wage hike

2.3 Fixed-Term Contracts (CDD) Rules

  • Written contract required
  • Trial period: 1 day per week worked; max 2 weeks if contract < 6 months, max 1 month if > 6 months
  • Renewal: Maximum 2 times
  • Waiting period between contracts for same employee/role: 1/3 of total duration if >14 days, 1/2 otherwise
  • Severance pay: 10% of gross salary
  • Exceptions: seasonal jobs, specific industries, resignations
CDD Severance Calculation: An employee working 3 months at 2,000 euros gross/month receives: 3 x 2,000 x 10% = 600 euros in severance.

Policy TypeDescription

Financial market imperativesAlign compensation with long-term results; larger share of deferred pay tied to company performance

2.5 Objectives of Compensation Policies

  • Towards employees: Keep employees motivated, reduce turnover, reward performance
  • With the labor market: Competitive packages to attract talent while considering industry standards
  • Internal strategy: Match the company's financial situation and long-term goals

2.6 Four Qualities of a Good Compensation Policy

A good compensation policy must be:
  1. Competitive: Matches or exceeds industry standards to attract and retain talent
  2. Flexible: Adapts to economic changes, organizational needs, and employee expectations
  3. Fair: Ensures employees are paid equitably for their work and responsibilities
  4. Motivating: Encourages higher performance by rewarding effort and results

3. Performance Evaluation

Evaluation: A continuous and strategic process of assessing, measuring, and developing individual and team performance in alignment with organizational goals (Lawler & McDermott, 2003; Aguinis, 2009; Biron, Farndale & Paauwe, 2011).

3.2 Designing an Evaluation System

Context factors to consider:

  • National and company culture
  • Size and organizational structure
  • Strategic choices and long-term plans
  • Existing HRM system

What to identify, measure, and develop:

  • Performance: How well employees achieve objectives
  • Behavior: How employees act, communicate, collaborate
  • Potential: Ability to grow, take on new responsibilities (succession planning)

3.4 Pitfalls of Annual Reviews

  • Evaluation period as a source of tension: Stress from anxiety, unclear expectations, fear of feedback. Avoid by preparing in advance, holding regular check-ins, keeping dialogue open.
  • The evaluation form: Generic or rigid forms reduce meaningful feedback. Forms should be guides that leave room for examples, strengths, challenges, and goals.
  • Communication of results: Only share results with those involved in employee development or who legally need access.

3.5 Career Development Interview

Mandatory for all employees since 2014, taking place at least every two years. It covers:

  • The employee's career plan
  • The commitment to training actions to be implemented
  • Joint evaluation of training effectiveness

3.6 Main Criticisms of Evaluation Systems

Key Criticisms:
  • Unfair & Imprecise: Judgments can be biased or inconsistent
  • Administrative Burden: Too much paperwork takes time from real improvement
  • Contradictory Goals: Tries to both control and develop employees
  • No Real Performance Boost: Rarely helps the organization actually improve
  • Demotivating: Can seriously hurt employee morale

3.7 Auclair Case Study — Compensation Allocation

Key Principles: Bonuses reward achieving objectives; salary increases reward efforts and motivate continued effort. In a cost-competitiveness strategy, variable pay (bonuses) is favored. Fairness requires: same bonus for achieving the same objective, and equal pay for equal skills regardless of seniority.
