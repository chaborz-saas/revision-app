Summary — HRM Ch 3: Training & Career Management

1. Training in HRM

Definition (Peretti, 2006): Training in HRM refers to the systematic process of equipping employees with the knowledge and skills necessary to perform their current jobs efficiently and prepare for future responsibilities, thereby enhancing organizational performance.

Training in HRM encompasses four main forms:

  1. Continuous professional training
  2. In-company training
  3. Digital training
  4. Outsourced training

2. Continuous Professional Training — Legal Frameworks

2.1 Personal Training Account (CPF)

  • Created in 2014
  • Allows all employees to benefit from training hours each year of work
  • Value: 500 euros per year (1 hour of training = 15 euros)
  • Ceiling: 5,000 euros maximum
  • Unused training hours remain credited for life, regardless of changes in employment situation

2.2 Professional Transition CPF

Replaced the Individual Leave for Training (CIF) in 2019.

  • Special case for CPF usage
  • Objective: Allow each employee to pursue certified training on their own initiative to change jobs or careers
  • Covers training costs and maintains at least 90% of pay in the first year
  • Eligibility for permanent employees: At least 24 months of work experience, including 12 months in the same company
  • Eligibility for fixed-term employees: At least 24 months of experience over the past 5 years, including 4 months on fixed-term contracts in the last 12 months

2.3 Validation of Prior Experience (VPE / VAE)

VPE: A system that allows any person engaged in working life to obtain all or part of a diploma or professional title by having their professional experience of at least one year validated by a jury.

3. In-Company Training

3.1 Company Obligations

Mandatory training contributions:
  • 0.55% of payroll for companies with up to 10 employees
  • 1% of payroll for companies with 11 or more employees
These amounts are paid to OPCOs (Skills Operators), which replaced OPCAs in 2019.

3.4 Digital Training — MOOCs

MOOCs (Massive Open Online Courses) represent a significant trend in digital training. Key aspects include:

  • Free or low-cost access to courses from major institutions
  • Popular platforms: Coursera, edX, FUN (France Universite Numerique)
  • Advantages: Accessibility, flexibility, wide range of subjects, self-paced learning
  • Challenges: High dropout rates, limited interaction, questions about certification value, difficulty assessing real learning outcomes

4. Career Management

Definition (Roger, 1991): A continuous process of learning and development through which individuals and organizations jointly plan and manage career paths to meet evolving personal aspirations and organizational objectives.

4.1 Objectives

For the company:

  • Manage jobs and skills (continuous education)
  • Develop cultural exchange and knowledge (for international corporations)
  • Stimulate individual motivation
  • Foster collaboration between units and enable sharing of management techniques

For the employees:

  • Improve the balance between contributions and rewards

5. Types of Mobility

TypeDescription
Vertical mobilityHierarchical level change (promotion or demotion)
Horizontal mobilityLateral move — change of function or job without hierarchical promotion
Geographical mobilityChange of work location at local, national, or international level

5.1 Key Questions for Career Policy

  • Which employees are concerned? (High Potentials / Talents / All talents)
  • What are the "normal" career development paths?
  • What mobility pace is desired?
  • Is internal mobility promoted, or are external competencies recruited?

5.2 Context Factors Influencing Mobility

Internal context:

  • Size (SMEs vs. large corporations)
  • Organizational structure (types of possible mobility, decision-making level)
  • Organizational culture
  • Strategic orientations (e.g., Consulting, Industry)
  • HR policy

External context (Livian, 2004):

  • Economic environment, competition
  • Educational system
  • Social norms
  • Role of public authorities and social partners (legislation, training schemes, collective agreements, job categories)

7. Primary Tools of Career Management

7.1 Tools to Meet Company Needs

  • Strategic Workforce Planning (GPEC): Anticipating future staffing needs
  • Job maps, occupational directories, competency frameworks: Mapping existing and needed skills
  • Career pathways: Structured progression routes

Priorities: Identify skills to be developed; encourage certain career paths.

7.2 Tools to Align Individual and Company Projects

  • Career committees
  • Career interviews
  • Development contracts
  • Professional interviews
  • Job boards
  • Talent / High Potential (HP) management

Priority: Co-define career paths.

7.3 Tools to Clarify Individual Projects

  • Individual assessments
  • Career counseling

Priorities: Enable employees to better understand themselves; facilitate achievement of individual projects.

7.4 Career Development Interview

Legal obligation since 2014. The interview covers:
  • The employee's professional development (qualifications, job position)
  • Monitoring of training, certification, salary progression, or professional advancement
  • Evolution of employability
  • Reflection on professional future, current position, and career plan
Generally conducted by an HR manager rather than the direct manager.

8. High Potentials & Talent Management

8.1 Who Are High Potentials?

  • Multiple definitions: High Potentials (HP) vs. Key People vs. Talents
  • Executives who have reached a certain level of responsibility within the company
  • Executives who are likely to reach this level of responsibility

8.2 Management of High Potentials

  • Offer rapid promotions
  • Retain individuals (incentives, benefits)
  • Test their performance level
  • Assess their commitment to the company

9. The Ference Matrix (1977)

The Ference Matrix: A talent management and succession planning tool that categorizes employees along two axes — Performance (low to high) and Potential (low to high) — to guide decisions about development, promotion, and retention.
Low PerformanceHigh Performance
High PotentialRising Stars: Employees with potential who need developmentStars: Top performers with high growth potential
Low PotentialDead Wood: Underperformers with limited potential for improvementPillars: Solid, reliable performers with limited growth potential

  • Geoffroy (Pillar): Head of Purchasing Division → Stay in role, mentor younger buyers, possible senior expert/advisor role
  • Julien (Rising Star): Workshop Manager → Management and administrative training; future role: Production Manager after skill development
  • Latifa (Rising Star): Project Manager → Coaching in project leadership and team management; future role: higher-level Project Manager
