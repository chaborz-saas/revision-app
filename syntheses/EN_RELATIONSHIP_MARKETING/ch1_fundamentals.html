Summary — Relationship Marketing Ch1: Fundamentals, Satisfaction & Customer Experience

This chapter covers the foundations of Relationship Marketing: from transactional to relational mindset, the definition and objectives of CRM, the link between satisfaction and loyalty, and the customer experience and journey.

1. Fundamentals of Relationship Marketing

1.1 What is Customer Relationship?

Customer Relationship Management focuses on 3 targets:

  • Current customers — retain and develop
  • Former customers — win back
  • Potential customers (prospects) — acquire selectively

The relationship is made up of all points of contact between the customer and the company (physical stores, website, mobile app, call center, social media, email, etc.).

1.2 Definition

Relationship Marketing: "All marketing activities aimed at establishing, developing and maintaining lasting relationships with the best customers" (Morgan & Hunt, 1994).

1.3 Two Competing Strategic Objectives

1. Acquisition of new customers: Expensive but necessary. Ideally: select the "right" prospects.
2. Loyalty of current customers: Increase profitability on current customers and extend the duration of the customer relationship.
Key tension: Both acquisition and loyalty compete for the same marketing budgets. Relationship Marketing must find the right balance between the two.

3. CRM & Technology

3.1 CRM vs. Relationship Marketing

CRM (Customer Relationship Management) has the same general meaning as Relationship Marketing, but with a more strategic (financial) and Information System connotation. The two terminologies can be used interchangeably.

3.2 The Financial Dimension of CRM

CRM adds a financial objective: create value for the company on each customer, developing the value of the company itself — the "customer equity", one of the company's key assets (alongside real estate, machines, inventory, etc.).

3.3 Technology in CRM

Technology is essential to Relationship Marketing:
  • Databases: internally constituted or purchased externally
  • Data storage and management
  • Automated market campaigns
  • Artificial Intelligence: data analysis and target decisions

4. The Tenuous Link Between Satisfaction and Loyalty

4.1 The Quality-to-Profit Chain

Quality → Satisfaction → Loyalty → Profit
From "Value FOR the customer" to "Value FROM the customer"

4.2 Satisfaction and Dissatisfaction

Satisfaction: A feeling of pleasure or pain that arises from the comparison between prior expectations and an actual experience.
Oliver's Simplified Model:
Satisfaction: Experience > Expectations
Dissatisfaction: Experience < Expectations
Beware of overpromising! If marketing creates expectations that exceed the actual experience, dissatisfaction results even with a good product.

4.3 Measuring Satisfaction

"If you don't measure it, you can't improve it" (Lord Kelvin):

  • Customer satisfaction surveys
  • Complaints (off and online)
  • Customer defection rate
  • Customer reviews on the Internet
  • Mystery shoppers

4.4 The 5 Benefits of Having Loyal Customers

  1. Retaining customers costs less than acquiring new ones
  2. A loyal customer buys more than a new one
  3. A loyal customer is regular (buys often)
  4. A loyal customer does positive word-of-mouth (becomes a promoter)
  5. A loyal customer helps reduce support costs (complaints & service costs)

4.5 Types of Loyalty

TypeDescription
Absolute loyaltyCustomer continues to buy the brand
Relative loyaltyBuys, but not exclusively. Share of Wallet < 100%. Goal is to increase this share.
Objective loyaltySimple observation of loyal behavior. May be passive (laziness, high switching costs) or forced (no alternatives, long-term commitment)
Subjective loyaltyPositive mental attitudes toward the brand. True emotional attachment.
Example: The arrival of Free on the French mobile market showed that Orange, SFR and Bouygues customers were not truly attached — they remained loyal (objective) only because there were no alternatives. When Free arrived, many switched.

4.6 The Satisfaction-Loyalty Correlation is Positive but Not Absolute

Key insight: In the USA, 90% of car owners are satisfied with their brand, but only 35% will buy the same brand again. Satisfaction does NOT automatically lead to loyalty.

Reasons why a satisfied customer leaves:

  • Solicitations from other brands
  • Satisfaction does not mean preference
  • Looking for discovery / variety
  • Low cost of change

4.7 Market Factors Affecting the Satisfaction-Loyalty Link

MarketSatisfaction-Loyalty ImpactReason
Automobiles, personal computersLowStrong competition, standard products, no switching cost
Local (wired) telephoneHighFew alternatives, high switching costs

5. Customer Experience & Customer Journey

5.1 Definitions

Customer Journey: The process followed by a customer through all the stages and contact points that make up the customer experience (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).
Customer Experience (CX): The sum of all experiences (functional, sensory, emotional, real or virtual) that a customer has in their relationship with a product, service, company or brand.

5.2 Customer Journey Frameworks

Do NOT focus Customer Journey Mapping only on purchasing:

FrameworkAssessment
3 stages (Before / Purchase / After)BAD — marketing bias, too simplistic
5 steps funnel (Awareness → Purchase)SIMPLISTIC — still purchase-centric
Full journey (Awareness → Consideration → Purchase → Use → Support → Advocacy → Elimination)GOOD — customer point of view, includes USE of product
Critical: Do not underestimate the USE phase (the reason why customers buy), maintenance, support, and elimination stages.

5.3 Consumer Culture Theory (Arnould & Thompson, 2005)

We consume not just for function, but also for the pleasure of consuming in the form of experiences:

  • Functional needs
  • Social needs
  • Ideological needs
  • Cultural needs
  • Symbolic and experiential needs

5.4 Customer Typology by Journey (Herhausen et al., 2019)

TypeDescriptionLoyalty Driver
(1) Physical shop addictsIn-store onlyLoyal when satisfied with product/service
(2) Online pragmaticsDigital only, few touchpoints
(3) Online intensivesDigital only, many touchpoints
(4) Multi-channel enthusiastsUse multiple channelsLoyal when satisfied AND journey is seamless
(5) O2O (online-to-offline)Research online, buy offline
Key takeaway: It is the CUSTOMER who chooses the route. Brands should serve a seamless experience whatever the route. A "Service Blueprint" diagram can help design satisfactory experiences for both customers and employees.

6. From Multichannel to Omnichannel

StageDescription
MultichannelSeveral independent points of contact
Cross-channelPoints of contact managed to streamline the journey (e.g., Click & Collect)
OmnichannelPoints of contact in synergy to maximize result. Physical and virtual touchpoints work together.
Today's customers say: "I-KWI-WAI-WIN!" — "I Know What I Want And I Want It Now!" They are demanding, informed, and expect seamless experiences across all channels.

7. Experiential Models

7.1 Schmitt's 5 SEMs (Strategic Experience Modules)

ModuleTypeDescription
SENSESensoryAppeals to the 5 senses
FEELAffectiveEmotional connections
THINKCognitiveCreative cognitive experiences
ACTBehavioralPhysical, behavioral engagement
RELATERelational/SocialSocial and community connections
Example: Peugeot creates a branded fragrance for its cars — a mix of SENSE (smell) and FEEL (emotional luxury). This combines sensory and affective experience modules.

7.2 Hetzel's Experiential Wheel (2002)

Five dimensions to create a memorable brand experience:

  1. Surprise the customer
  2. Offer the extraordinary
  3. Stimulate the 5 senses
  4. Create a link with the customer
  5. Immerse the customer in the brand universe
Example: The DILL experience by LIDL transformed the discount retailer's image by using surprise, extraordinary offerings, sensory stimulation, emotional links, and immersion in a reimagined brand universe.
