Summary — Relationship Marketing Ch3: Loyalty Programs & Customer Value Strategy

This chapter covers the types and effectiveness of loyalty programs, key loyalty KPIs, the strategic shift from revenue to profit, the CRM operational framework, and the Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) approach.

1. Loyalty Programs 2. Types 3. Levers 4. Loyalty KPIs 5. Limits 6. Strategic CRM 7. CLV 8. Targeting

1. What is a Loyalty Program?

Loyalty Program (LP): Marketing actions aimed at rewarding and encouraging loyal behavior (Meyer-Waarden, 2002). An integrated system of marketing actions aimed at rewarding and encouraging customer behavior and attitude through incentives (Kang et al., 2015).
Synthesis: A set of actions or benefits offered to certain customers. The objective is to increase the amount purchased, the profitability of the company, and to create a lasting and profitable relationship.

4. Main KPIs to Monitor Loyalty

4.1 Attrition Rate

Attrition Rate = Number of customers lost over the period / Number of customers at the start of the period

Example: 10,000 customers in January, 9,000 in December
Attrition = (10,000 - 9,000) / 10,000 = 10%

4.2 Loyalty Rate (Retention Rate)

Retention Rate = 100% - Attrition Rate
Example: 100% - 10% = 90% of customers remained loyal

4.3 Net Promoter Score (NPS)

NPS measures positive recommendation net of negative recommendations.
NPS = % Promoters - % Detractors
Based on the question: "Would you recommend this brand to friends and family on a scale of 0 to 10?"
ScoreCategoryDescription
0-6DetractorsUnhappy customers, risk of negative WOM
7-8Passives/NeutralsSatisfied but unenthusiastic. Vulnerable to competition, difficult to convert.
9-10PromotersVery loyal to the brand, positive WOM
Benchmark: An NPS of 50% or more is considered good. NPS around 75% is excellent.

4.4 Customer Lifetime

Customer Lifetime = 1 / Attrition Rate

Example: If 10% leave per year, average lifetime = 1/10% = 10 years.
After 10 years with no new acquisitions, active customers fall to zero.

5. The Limits of Loyalty Programs

  • Their cost is often underestimated
  • Despite millions of members, effectiveness remains ambiguous
  • Imitation from competitors gradually destroys the initial competitive advantage
  • Often unclear whether customers are loyal to the program incentives or to the brand itself

6. Strategic CRM: From Revenue to Profit

6.1 The Paradigm Shift

Traditional Marketing: The customer is king, a source of income.
CRM: The profitable customer is the source of profit. Not all customers are equally valuable. (Model of Cova & Cova)

6.3 CRM Purposes

  1. Improve contact quality: Better welcome, guide, and advise (Corporate Image)
  2. Target, target, target: Orient resources toward potential customers, reduce off-target costs
  3. Seize opportunities: Customer service calls as up-sell opportunities, recover valuable customers
  4. Keep them loyal: Cheaper than acquisition, reduce churn, anticipate with personalized retention
  5. Create value: On each customer and therefore on the company itself (customer equity)

6.5 Customer Orientation Score (COS) — Daniel Ray, 2014

COS measures the Perception Gap between employees and customers. On average, 66% of employees believe they show consideration toward customers, but only 42% of customers agree (24-point gap). Benchmark for acceptable COS: 55%.

6.6 CRM and IT

CRM is a major IT challenge but must remain under control of operational marketers and sales people. IT's role: digitization, automation, interconnection. The strategy should dictate what the CRM system does, not the reverse. Major players: Salesforce, SAP, Oracle, Microsoft CRM.

7. Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

CLV: The present value of the flow of future profits (or future cash flows) expected from transactions with a customer over the long term. Uses the NPV (Net Present Value) financial method to account for uncertainty.
CLV = Customer Value per year x Customer Lifetime

Customer Value per year = Customer Revenue - (Acquisition Costs + Loyalty Costs)

Customer Lifetime = 1 / Attrition Rate

Principle: Total purchases - Acquisition & retention expenses = Customer Value (NPV)
Basic principle of CLVM: You have to keep a customer for a long time to recoup the initial acquisition expenses!
The "Elevator Company" business model: Acquisition via contract (Year 1: 50,000 EUR installation, cost 55,000 EUR = loss), then recurring maintenance revenue (2,000 EUR/year, cost 1,000 EUR/year = profit). The initial loss is recouped over years of maintenance. Other examples: boilers, printers, phone subscriptions, newspaper subscriptions = "Hard financed by Soft."

8. Targeting Marketing Actions

8.1 The Targeting Challenge

Marketing actions (promotions, visits, events, gifts) are costly with limited budgets. Key questions:

  • On which targets should we spend them?
  • Should we develop relationships with all interesting targets?
  • For the entire customer group or only certain customers?

8.2 Warning: Poorly Targeted Acquisition Destroys Value

Value destruction occurs when acquisition offers (at high cost) also apply to existing customers:
  • Existing customers substitute the promotion for their old contract (cannibalization)
  • Existing customers "wake up" and look at the market, potentially going to competition (the "Wake-up effect")

8.3 Measuring Loyalty Action Effectiveness

Example: A customer portfolio with 30% termination/year (lifetime = 3.3 years).
Target 20% of customer base with retention actions (Loyalty Rate = 20%).
Effectiveness Rate of chosen actions = 50%.
Result: 10% of customers retained, termination drops to 20%/year.
New customer lifetime = 1/20% = 5 years.
Gain = 1.7 years x annual cash flow.
Loyalty is managed by mastering both Targeting AND Effectiveness of actions.
