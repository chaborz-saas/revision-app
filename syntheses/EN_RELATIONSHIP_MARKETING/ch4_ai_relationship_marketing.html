Summary — Relationship Marketing Ch4: AI & Relationship Marketing

This chapter explores how Generative AI, Intelligent Agents, and different levels of AI are transforming relationship marketing. It examines in-store technology, the debate on AI and "real" relationships, and the concept of collaborative intelligence.

1.1 Applications of Generative AI in Customer Relationships

  • Hyper-personalized email marketing: Dynamic subject lines, tone matching
  • AI-generated customer service responses: With memory of past conversations
  • Voice agents for loyalty programs: E.g., "Alexa, what's my Carrefour balance?"

2. Intelligent Agents

Intelligent Agents = Autonomous systems that take into account their environment (e.g., camera), make decisions independently (e.g., face recognition), act, and learn from experience.

2.1 Examples in Marketing

  • Monoprix: Uses a generative chatbot trained on FAQ + CRM data
  • Real-time newsletter personalization
  • Conversational AI answering customer queries
  • Recommendation engines: Proactively offer support or deals via push notifications

3. The Three Levels of AI (Huang et al., 2023)

AI progresses through three levels of intelligence. At each level, AI first augments and then replaces human intelligence.
LevelTypeFunctionExamples
AMechanical AIExecutes tasksChatbots, dynamic pricing
BThinking AIAnalyzes dataRecommender systems, predictive churn models
CFeeling AIRecognizes emotionsSentiment analysis, facial emotion detection
Case studies discussed in class:
  • Sephora ColorIQ: Scans skin tone to match foundation shades (Thinking AI — analyzes data to make recommendations)
  • L'Oreal Modiface: Virtual try-on for cosmetics using AR (Mechanical + Thinking AI)
  • ITRI smart store (Taiwan): 24-hour "grab and go" using face recognition and sensors (Mechanical + Thinking AI, approaching Feeling AI)
  • Orange Customer Service: Combining AI automation with human empathy (hybrid approach)
  • Amazon: Multiple AI uses: recommendation engine, voice assistant (Alexa), logistics optimization, personalized marketing

4. Using In-Store Technology to Support Relationship Marketing

Key framework (Grewal et al., Journal of Retailing 2023): In-store technology should be analyzed along two dimensions:
  • Efficiency: Making processes faster, cheaper, smoother (for customers AND employees)
  • Enhancement: Making experiences richer, more engaging, more memorable
Managerial lesson: Technology must serve BOTH efficiency and enhancement to maximize impact on customer relationships.

5. Can AI Create "Real" Relationships?

Central debate: Can AI create real relationships with customers (and humans in general)? What makes a relationship "real"?

Key considerations:

  • AI can simulate empathy but does not truly feel emotions
  • Customers may value speed and convenience over genuine emotional connection
  • Trust is a critical factor — can customers trust an AI agent?
  • The line between "authentic" and "simulated" relationships is blurring
  • Different customer segments may have different expectations

5.1 Benefits and Drawbacks of AI in Relationship Marketing

BenefitsDrawbacks
For businessesCost reduction, scalability, 24/7 availability, data-driven decisions, personalization at scaleImplementation costs, loss of human touch, data privacy risks, technology dependency
For customersFaster service, personalized experiences, 24/7 availability, consistent qualityPrivacy concerns, lack of empathy, frustrating when AI fails, loss of genuine human interaction

6. Collaborative Intelligence: Humans + AI

Collaborative Intelligence = Combining human strengths and AI strengths in CRM.
Human StrengthsAI Strengths
Empathy, context understanding, intuitionSpeed, volume processing, pattern recognition
Emotional connection, moral values24/7 availability, consistency
Creative problem-solving, judgmentData analysis at scale, prediction
Key principle: AI augments and complements human intelligence. Be an architect of combined AI + human use, not just a basic user of AI. Relationship marketers must become architects of hybrid intelligence.

6.1 Schumpeter's Creative Destruction

Joseph Schumpeter's theory: Capitalism is never stationary. New technologies, markets, and forms of organization constantly revolutionize the economic structure from within — "incessantly destroying the old one, incessantly creating a new one." This applies to AI's transformation of CRM: old practices are being destroyed and replaced by new AI-powered approaches.

7. Conclusions: Technology, AI & Relationship Marketing

Four key takeaways:
  1. AI is transforming relationship marketing — but NOT totally replacing humans in the long term
  2. Trust is a key success factor for AI in most domains, including Relationship Marketing
  3. Technology must be aligned with BOTH the customer journey AND the employee journey
  4. Relationship marketers must become architects of hybrid intelligence
